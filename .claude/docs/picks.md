## What the surface is

One hash — `#my-lisst` — wearing TWO COSTUMES. On the desktop it is **My Lisst**: a grid of picked boards plus the long inquiry form (see the inquiry-form entry in [contact-composer.md](contact-composer.md)), a quiet page beside the real contact stage, which lives in the deck drawer. On the phone it is **YOUR PICKS**, and since react round 6 of 2026-08-12 it is not a page beside the deck — **it IS the deck** (Dylan: "the deck drawer should away on mobile =("). The takeover case retired; this is where the phone holds, prices and sends its picks. **THE FORK IS ONE VARIABLE:** `const M = dockActive()` at the top of `renderLisst`, and every branch below reads it. `dockActive()` is `matchMedia("(max-width: 940px)").matches`, and **a resize across that boundary mid-page RE-DRESSES it** — the 940 change listener calls `renderLisst()` when the hash is `my-lisst`, so a visitor who crosses gets the other costume rather than a half-page. `closeLb` also re-renders on the way out (lisst membership may have changed inside an inspection).

**THE GOVERNING RULE OF THE WHOLE SURFACE (Dylan, 2026-08-15): a pick is either IN YOUR HAND or ON THE TABLE, never both.** The bar's mini stack and this page tell one story between them, and every mechanism below — the deal, the `stackDealt` flag, the repopulate-on-route — exists to keep that true.

## The stack in the bar

`renderPicks()` writes `#hpicks` (inside `#picon`, whose own `#my-lisst` href is the click and the swipe-door's visible twin). Each mini is `<i class="hpick" style="--i:N;z-index:M">` around the REAL `JCARDS.framev3` template via `jmeta` — **a mini is a full 232px card under a transform, not a small card**, which is the fact the first deal-fly crashed on (below). `HPICK_MAX = 4`; past the cap `.hcount` carries the rest alone. **THE STACK FACES LEFT AND FANS BEHIND ITSELF** (his sketch, commit `THE STACK FACES LEFT, FANS BEHIND ITSELF, AND CAPS AT FOUR`): newest FIRST, `--i` stepping the diagonal, and an explicit DESCENDING z ladder written here because paint order used to invert the fan for free — front-left means FIRST on top, and `renderPicks` is the only place that knows the count. **Newest-first is not decoration, it is what keeps the ceremony honest:** the deal takes the FRONT mini and the page's grid renders newest-first too, so the mini that leaves IS the card that lands. **THE EMPTY SEAT IS THE SAME OBJECT** (2026-08-16, his react — "the same mini card the stacks get instead of the blank flat square... with a 0 in the count slot"): the empty branch renders the fv3 template's own boardless shell, `.d7f.mtblank` (panel + inner frame + gap dots + an empty stage), plus `.hcount` reading a quiet `0` — this superseded BOTH the hand-drawn blank and the no-count-when-empty rule. **THE MINIS LOAD EAGER** (his catch: "a routed board turns to a blank mini in the hand shaped section") — the template's lazy `img` is a 0×0 box until it loads and a zero box never intersects the viewport, so the lazy loader only ever fired when the same URL happened to be warm from the current page's own grid; the bar is permanent chrome, so `renderPicks` forces `loading = "eager"` on every one.

**`stackDealt` + `stackDealtAway()` keep the hand-or-table rule from fighting itself.** On the phone's picks page the stack renders EMPTY — the ceremony dealt it away — so any re-render landing while you are still there (removing a pick, say) must agree rather than quietly refill the bar. But ARRIVING must leave the stack full, because the ceremony needs cards to deal from: **only a COMPLETED deal sets the flag, and every `route()` clears it.** `renderPicks` is also re-run by the ceremony itself the moment the last card lands, so the two ways of reaching an empty bar are the same code — otherwise a freshly-dealt stack kept a count hanging in the corner beside nothing. **DO NOT TIDY `stackDealtAway`'s TERM ORDER:** `stackDealt` MUST be tested first, because `dockActive` is a `const` arrow declared ~2500 lines later and `renderPicks` runs at BOOT through `lisstTab()` — reaching `dockActive()` in that first render is a TDZ ReferenceError that aborts the whole script and kills the site silently. The short-circuit is the guard. `lisstTab()` is the one update path (the `#nav-lisst` label, `#pcnt-d`, `#pcnt`, then `renderPicks`).

## The departure — leaving before you arrive

**`leaveToPicks()` (his ask: "regardless of where you are on the site, if you click the stack in the header, the rest of the site below it fades out").** The picks route is the ONE route that leaves before it arrives: `body.pxleave` drains the page under the bar for `PX.leave` (300ms, paired with the CSS transition), THEN the hash flips. **The bar is deliberately exempt** — it is what you tapped, and it is where the cards are about to come from. Desktop and reduced motion set the hash directly. **ALL THREE DOORS ROUTE THROUGH IT** so the page can never arrive two different ways: the `#picon` stack tap (the anchor keeps its real `#my-lisst` href as the fallback for no-JS, desktop and reduced motion — the handler only claims the phone's ceremonial path), the browse's LEFT-SWIPE commit (`swo`, committing mid-move at `SWC_TH` so the page starts arriving under the still-down finger — the swipe is caught, not answered at release; rightward is never claimed, Safari's edge nav owns it; home never arms, per the showroom law), and the case's MSG bar. On the way in, `body.pxleave` is dropped WITHOUT a transition — the rule is scoped to the class so removing it snaps — because the arriving page holds itself hidden through `.picks-deal` and fading it in over the top of its own ceremony would state everything twice.

## THE PICKS CEREMONY (`playPicksDeal`)

Built 2026-08-15/16 across ten-plus react rounds after three variants were collided and retired into it (`THE PICKS ARRIVAL — three variants to collide` → `THE PICKS CEREMONY — the arrival becomes a sequence (thread 4, his full spec)`; variant C's state-and-pulse keyframe survives as the masthead's entrance). **CALLED FROM EXACTLY ONE PLACE — `route()`'s `my-lisst` branch, and last in it**, after `lockCardScale()` has sized the cards and after the instant landing, because every landing rect the deal measures depends on both. NOT on a pick-removal re-render (re-dealing the whole deck because you dropped one board is the strip's redraw complaint again), not on `· done ·`, not on the lightbox's soft close back onto the page (you never left it), not on the 940 boundary re-dress. Desktop and reduced motion return before `.picks-deal` is ever added, so the held-states can never apply and nothing can be stranded hidden.

**THE BEATS:** `.picks-deal` + `body.dealing` on → the masthead states and pulses (`PX.mast` 660) → one card at a time flies out of the stack into its slot → its offer box answers before the next launches → MESSAGE stamps → SEND stamps → the page rides home and a caret starts blinking in the first offer box. **`alive()` is the ceremony's licence** — its token is newest (`dealN`), its DOM is still attached, and the hash is still `my-lisst`; any of the three false and it stands down silently at the next await.

**`dealCard` — THE FLY MUST BE BUILT AT THE DESTINATION'S SIZE.** The first cut launched it at the header mini's box rect and flew a black slab of garbage (Dylan: "getting a weird animation on the card flight"), because `.hpick .d7f` is a full 232px card under `transform: scale(.1078)` inside an 8px clipping window — an 8px-wide fly rendered a whole template card into 8px. The fix is `pickCatch` run backwards: build at the DESTINATION card's size, CENTRE the launch rect on the mini, and let an inner `.deal-grow` wrapper carry the growth from mini scale to full. **The wrapper is load-bearing** — `bayFlight` writes the fly's own transform every frame and would overwrite a scale set there, so the two transforms must live on different elements. The launch is measured off the mini's ARTWORK (`.d7f`), not its box: the box is the 8px clipping window, the `.d7f` inside it is the 22px card the eye actually sees. The fly wears `.lisst-card` so it lands DRESSED against a dressed card (rest == rest, the soft-return law — a bare fly would flash at the handoff), and there is no `drain` (the deck's flights drain because they land on a REST card; this is the opposite case).

**THE STACK IS DEALT AWAY, AND THE MINIS ARE THE CARDS** (commit `THE STACK IS DEALT AWAY — the minis are the cards`; his react: "have the deal happen from the top card... and have it stay gone on deal... a move anywhere else should repopulate them"). Every deal takes the FRONT standing mini — `.hp-out` slides it clear, then `.hp-gone` COLLAPSES its box 90ms later so the stack really shrinks (measured 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0) — and it does not come back. **"Front" needs no bookkeeping:** the boxes are slivers and each card's artwork spills over its neighbour, so taking the first standing one reveals the next as the new front for free, no re-render and no z rewrite. An earlier cut had the mini RETURN at touchdown ("absent only while its card is in the air") — honest per-frame, and it lost the thing he was after. `clearMinis()` at the top is the belt: an interrupted deal must not leave the stack half-dealt, and `route()` repopulates on the way out.

**THE GRID RUNS NEWEST-FIRST ON THE PHONE** (2026-08-15, his react: "it does read off. can we flip the deal order to match the minis"). `order = M ? [...myLisst].reverse() : myLisst`. **Flipping the GRID rather than the deal's direction is the load-bearing half:** it keeps the page filling top-left → down in one walk, where dealing the existing order backwards would have filled bottom-up and made the page walk down and then back up again. The STACK is not flipped — newest-on-top is what makes `pickCatch`'s landing read as the confirmation of an add, and that costs more than it would buy. Desktop keeps `myLisst` order, and every string (refs, tags, the letter) still reads in `myLisst` order on both sides.

**THE CLOCKS live in `PX`, and they have been cut in both directions.** The card deal went ×2 on 2026-08-16 ("lets speed up the card deal x2", after an earlier 25% cut): `hand` 72 · `deal` 233 · `settle` 100 · `ask` 130 — and `hand` is a THREE-PLACE beat (the mini's own `hpOut` .09s and the `hp-gone` timeout track it; move one, move all three). The CLOSING went the other way the same day — **the deal is quick, the statements are slow**: stamps .62 → .92s, `tail` 1000 (SEND lands as MESSAGE's pop settles), `rest` 1600, `home` 1240. `paLand` is RETIRED (a .35→1 landing ramp read as a flash; the fly holds its last frame onto a dressed card — rest == rest needs no ramp). **THE WORD POP replaced the breathe after one react** ("grow big once and settle... more like the bounce message copied gets"): `paWordPop`, .55s at a .82s delay INSIDE the .92s `paStamp` — **a knob PAIR, move together** — one-shot per landing, `--pop` peaking 1.45 on the label and 1.2 on the button text (which must stay inside the slab's 24px side padding), on the independent `scale` property, and `.picks-deal`-scoped because **a pop is a landing's answer; a re-render just places things.**

**THE SCROLL IS TWO DRIVEN RIDES, NOTHING NATIVE.** `glide(top, ms)` is rAF + easeInOut with every write `behavior: "instant"` — `html`'s smooth scroll turns per-frame writes into mush (route()'s own lesson), and a native smooth scroll owns its ~.4s clock so its duration cannot be authored. **Ride one is ONE CONTINUOUS PAGE-DOWN** (round 5: "have the scroll to the bottom be one continuous motion. time the scroll from deal start to message box entrance"): the per-row walks and the closing seat scroll were FOLDED INTO a single glide launched with the first card, its length `dealTotal × PX.rideX` (1.5 — at 1:1 the eased middle outpaced the cards, his round-6 catch), ending at the seat that leaves SEND 150px of toolbar room. That end seat is measurable BEFORE the first card flies because **ceremony layout never moves** (held parts use visibility/opacity and NEVER `display` — otherwise every measured landing rect is a lie), and `bayFlight` re-measures landings per frame so cards land true on a moving page. **`walkTo`, `PX.walk` and `PX.seat` are DELETED — do not resurrect the stepped walks.** MESSAGE stamps mid-ride, in frame by construction since the ride is headed at the seat that frames it; the `await ride` sits BELOW `msg.in` so SEND still waits for the parked seat plus the full tail. Ride two is the ride home, ending in `showOfferCaret(inner)`.

**THE DEAL SHIELD:** `body.dealing::after`, a transparent fixed pseudo over EVERYTHING including the bar (the stack mid-deal is dealing itself) at **z 6700 — a new top rung above the topbar's 6600.** `touch-action: none` stops iOS panning, and clicks resolve to BODY (a pseudo's events target its origin element) so no handler ever fires. Up with the gate, DOWN the moment SEND lands — **the page is yours the instant SEND appears, and its pop plays on an unlocked page** — with `route()` clearing it as the belt. `.picks-deal` itself comes off at the very end so an interrupted or mis-stepped ceremony can never strand a part invisible; the `.in`/`.dealt` classes are inert once it is gone.

**THE 150px BUFFER IS A PAIR.** The ceremony used to end wherever the last walk left it, dropping SEND behind Safari's floating toolbar; the ride now seats it with 150px underneath — which needed `.picks-send`'s bottom buffer to grow 100 → 160, because a buffer is only worth what the page can actually scroll to. **The send-lock's own clamp moved 220 → 280 with it; they differ by a constant 120 — move one, move the other.**

## The page's own verbs

**THE PICKS-CARD TAP IS A FLIP, NEVER THE LIGHTBOX** (round 5, his call: "tapping a card in the picks page should flip its board / move the caret to its offer box - shouldnt open lightbox view"). On the phone this page IS the inspection — every pick was already inspected on its way in. A card tap flips the board to its canva-pair other face (`BOT(b)`; no pair means no flip — originals/resale ride the reshoot interim) as squash-swap-expand on the independent `scale` property (`transform` belongs to `lockCardScale`), with both faces WARMED at render so the swap never shows a loading face, and walks the drawn caret to that card's own offer box. A tap on the offer BOX walks the caret without flipping — the box is offer furniture, not the board. **THE DEBOUNCE IS A SELF-EXPIRING TOKEN** (`img.dataset.flipT`, the `slideT` law), not a cleared flag: a cleanup timer missed because Safari froze the tab mid-flip would lock the card forever. Desktop `.lisst-card` keeps `openLb`.

**THE BENCH — THE ✕ IS TWO TAPS** (round 7, the finale; commit `THE BENCH (his round 7) — the ✕ is two taps, and the message is curated`; his words: "on first tap the card should dim and the offer box below it should exit. the x on the card should now be red. tap the red x - remove it"). `benchSet(id, card, cell, on)` does four things at once: the card's ART dims to .38 (**the `.d7f`, never the `.scard` — parent opacity would dim the armed ✕ too**), the offer box slides off toward ITS OWN COLUMN's screen edge (`bx-l`/`bx-r`, chosen by `cellGoL(cell)` off the cell's real midpoint) **while KEEPING ITS SPACE** — the bench never reflows, only the removal's re-render does — the ✕ arms RED, and the board's chip leaves the MESSAGE line (`.ptag.off` by `data-id`). The armed red ✕ removes for real: the whole cell rides off its side (`cell-out-l`/`-r`, double-tap guarded by `cell.dataset.leaving`), then the re-render reflows. **A tap ANYWHERE on the dimmed card brings everything back** — no flip, no caret walk; a benched card's only answer is returning. **THE LETTER IS CURATED WITH THE LINE:** `bdmLines`/`bdmText`/`bdmCopy` take an optional skip set and the press passes `benched`, so the clipboard, the subject and both door hrefs filter in ONE seat and the same-string law holds curated (typed offers survive benching). **PER-VISIT STATE — every `route()` clears the bench**, because `myLisst` is the bookmark shelf (his frame: "book mark selections and then chose the ones you are interested in") and the ceremony always deals a full hand. The accidental-remove guard is the whole point ("finding the card again on mobile might be difficult"); desktop keeps its one-tap disc, there is no bench markup up there.

**THE OFFER BOX** (`.pick-ask` → `.pa-l` label + `.pick-in` input) rides a WRAPPER CELL beside the card, **never inside it** — `lockCardScale` clamps the `.scard`'s height to the scaled card, so an in-card row is orphaned outside the box and paints over whatever follows (bitten on the first cut). The cell is the grid child; `.scard` styling and `lockCardScale`'s descendant query both survive the extra layer. Every keystroke lands in `bdmComments[id].text` — **the desktop composer's own ask string, so the same-string law crosses the 940 boundary** — and is prefilled back on every render, so the page and the letter can never disagree. Geometry after four rounds: 78% of card width, CENTRED under the card (round 4 walked back round 3's left rail), margin-top 14, the drawn kit on WHOLE PIXELS. The caret is DRAWN, not focused (`showOfferCaret(inner, target)`) — focusing would throw the iOS keyboard over the page the ceremony just laid out — it WALKS to whichever card was last named, there is only ever one, and it is seated off the real input because the row is a flex box whose label width moves with the copy.

## The send flow on this page

`picksSend` (phone, and only when there are tags) is the block the ceremony ends on: `.picks-msg` — the MESSAGE box holding `im interested in` plus one `.ptag` black chip per pick, each carrying its `data-id` so the bench can drop it without a re-render — over `.picks-copy`, the stacked-verb press. **THE PRESS COPIES FIRST** (`bdmCopy(benched)` rides the REAL gesture, the popup-jail family), then fills both door hrefs with the same letter — belt and braces, since platforms that strip prefill still get the paste — then buzzes and enters the takeover. **THE SEND TAKEOVER (2026-08-13, eight react rounds; full round-by-round record in [mobile.md](mobile.md)):** the first press measures the message box's viewport seat BEFORE anything moves, clamps it as `--lockY` so `· done ·` fits below and the doors + banner fit above, freezes the page (`body.sendlock` + `body.style.top = -scrollY`, the iOS-proof lock), collapses `.picks-above` upward, and adds `.sendmode` — after which `.picks-copied` ("message copied") banners at the top, `.picks-paste` ("send it @") seats itself, and `.picks-doors` reveals after the pulse. **Every seat in that cluster is COMPUTED, NEVER MEASURED** — press-time `getBoundingClientRect` lies on iOS, because the tap re-expands Safari's toolbar and fixed-element rects lurch mid-transition (bitten: the line landed ~70pt low on the sim while the same math was pixel-exact in the pane). **A RE-PRESS IN SENDMODE IS THE FAILED-PASTE RECOVERY** — it re-copies and re-pops the banner instead of replaying the collapse. `.picks-door`s are the envelope back face's own chips, their artwork CLONED at render out of the live `#bb-reddit`/`#bb-mail` markup by `doorArt` (one artwork, two seats, edited together by construction — the bay markup is static so the source exists whether or not the composer ever opened); destinations come from `CONTACT` in `data.js`, never from markup. `· done ·` calls `sendUnlock()` + `renderLisst()` + an instant scroll home — a fresh render, so the lock, the ridden-out stack and the banner all reset while typed offers survive in `bdmComments`. **`sendUnlock()` also runs at the top of every `route()`**: a topbar escape mid-takeover must not leave the page frozen. **THE OFFER FIELD'S KEYBOARD** is its own fix and it cannot be tested on the sim (which runs a hardware keyboard, so the visual viewport never shrinks) — a capture-phase `pointerdown` seats the field in the upper third BEFORE focus, with `--vvTop` tracking `visualViewport.offsetTop` under `body.kbd` as the net; confirmed on Dylan's phone 2026-08-16. Desktop's counterpart is the `.inq` form (contact-composer.md) — a completely separate branch, and the only thing on this site that POSTs.
